# Email Rewrite

Email drafting is usually iterative: the user changes one thing per turn. The job is to make exactly that change, keep everything else intact, and hand back one clean version.

## Workflow

1. **Find the current draft.** If the user pasted a version, that version is the source of truth, including their own edits and wording. Never revert to an earlier draft or reintroduce lines they removed.
2. **Isolate the one change requested.** Apply only that. Leave every other sentence word for word. The only extra edits allowed are fixing clear typos and adding minimal connecting words so the changed part reads naturally.
3. **Respect scope.** "Make that sentence kinder" means edit that sentence only. "A line before the sign-off" means before the sign-off, not in the P.S.
4. **Deliver one version**, the full email, not fragments and not multiple options, unless the user asks for options.

## Content rules

- **Facts stay exact.** Names, spellings, dates, project names and roles come from the user. Check the sign-off name against the user's actual name every time; typos in names are the easiest mistake to make.
- **Do not invent.** No new commitments, reasons, achievements or promises. If a small addition was needed to make the email work (for example a thank-you line), keep it minimal and flag it in one short line after the draft so the user can cut it.
- **Write as the user,** in first person, matching their register. Do not make them sound more formal or more casual than they asked.

## Tone moves

- **Kinder / more forgiving:** soften the framing, remove anything that reads as blame or a complaint, keep the substance. Example: "has not been made clear" becomes "is still to be decided, and that is completely fine".
- **Stronger / more professional:** replace hedges ("honestly", "maybe", "seems") with confident, specific statements. A closing should state the outcome and the next step, and can include a brief thanks.
- **Less uncertain:** if the uncertainty is about the user's own plans, rewrite it as a plan or decision. Keep uncertainty only where it is real and outside the user's control.
- **Removing pressure:** put the onus on the sender, not the reader. "No pressure from our end", not "no pressure on your end".
- **Flow:** merge choppy sentences, cut repeated words (watch for the same word four times in a few lines, such as "flexible"), and keep paragraphs short.

## Structure

Greeting, context, main point, plans or details, one closing paragraph before the sign-off, sign-off. Use a P.S. only when asked, and keep it for side information. The conclusion belongs in the body, not the P.S. No bullet points in an email unless the user asks.

## Style defaults

Australian English, no em dashes, plain words, short paragraphs. Any style preferences the user has saved or stated override these defaults.

## Output

- Give the full email once. If a compose or draft card tool is available, use it with a single variant and a subject line, and do not paste the body again in chat.
- After the draft, add one or two sentences: what changed, anything you added that the user did not say, and anything worth double checking (for example, a word that might not match what they meant).
- Ask at most one question, and only if the instruction is truly ambiguous. Otherwise take the most likely reading and state it in one line.
